Senior Capstone

(See “Capstone” tab for more)

I completed my senior capstone as part of the “Wildlife Management and Conservation” course under the guidance of Dr. Jenny Duggan and her then masters student Sara Goel. We partnered with California State Parks to monitor the effects of campsite construction at the Fort Ord Dunes State Beach. This project began in Spring 2025 and will continue until after construction is completed by future “Wildlife Management and Conservation” classes. Field methods varied from trail cameras, live captures (as shown in my profile picture), and acoustic sampling. While no formal hypothesis testing was completed, we compared and contrasted different population indices both across sites and across time. In addition to a written report submitted to California State Parks, our team presented our research as a slideshow to the Applied Environmental Science Spring 2026 Capstone Festival.

Studies Designed

1. Dusky-footed Woodrat Habitat Analysis

(See “Woodrat Study” tab for poster and full report)

In my Ecology class, we were tasked with designing and carrying out a study focused on a habitat of our choice. My team and I decided to focus on Neotoma fuscipes (dusky-footed woodrat) in the oak woodland and coastal scrubland. Given the importance of their nests for other nearby species, information about where the woodrat builds their nests was beneficial for the manager of University of Santa Cruz’s Fort Ord Nature Reserve. With the help of the manager, 30 random points in both habitat types were determined. My team and I then went to each point and recorded the size of each nest within a three meter radius as well as the size and species of vegetation the nest was under. I lead the statistical analysis because of my statistic minor and can provide my R project and markdown file if wanted. For the Ecology class only a poster was required of our research project. We were also fortunate enough to present the poster as guests for the Department of Applied Environmental Science Fall 2024 Capstone Festival at our university. For my Introduction to G.I.S class, we were tasked with completing an analysis using spatial data of our choice. I decided to revisit my woodrat data using ArcGIS Pro and focus on canopy cover and vegetation type. Both a written report and an ArcGIS Pro layout were required for this class.

2. Purple Urchin Experiment

(See “Purple Urchin Study” tab for full report)

The final project for my Experiment Design and Analysis class involved designing and carrying out our own experiment. My partner and I decided to explore the relationship between Strongylocentrotus purpuratus (purple sea urchin) count and tide level. The Monterey Bay region has a variety of beaches with rocky areas which allowed us to utilize a randomized complete block design. We then randomly selected a beach for each day an counted urchins along a randomly placed transect at both zero feet and one foot tide level. After collecting our data, and checking the pools for our favorite nudibranchs, a type I ANOVA test was completed looking at the urchin count while considering both tide level and location. The R project and markdown file can be provided if interested. Both the R code and report were required for this class.

Graduate Writing Assessment

(See “Ringtail Case Study” tab for more)

A requirement at my university for undergraduates is a GWAR class, or Graduate Writing Assessment Requirement. It involves a major writing assignment relative to your major. There are two courses for Biology majors that offered different assignments. I decided to complete the course that resulted in a case study since there was more flexibility in the topic of choice than the other course. Given my interest in mammals, I wrote my case study on the preservation of Bassariscus astutus (ringtails) in San Diego County. In addition to the required sections, a data analysis of roadkill frequencies using California Roadkill Observation System data was completed by me as supplemental information.
